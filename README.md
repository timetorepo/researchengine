## What Research Engine is

Research Engine is a research workspace that runs on **your** computer. It is a library, a study desk and a writing room in one place: ask a question and get an answer that shows where it came from, drop in a paper and get a study guide, keep notes, write drafts, send a team of assistant agents over a topic, and turn anything you learned into something you can share.

It is made for people who think for a living — **researchers, students, teachers, analysts, journalists, writers** — and for anyone who would rather not hand their unfinished thinking to a company. Nothing you write is uploaded anywhere by us. There is no us: you run the app, so the files are yours, in a folder you can open, back up, or delete.

You do not need to be technical to use it. You need one thing to get real AI answers — an account with an AI provider of your choice (or a model running on your own machine) — and if you have neither yet, the app still works out of the box with a built-in offline model so you can look around first.

**Download it, open it, start asking.** No sign-up, no subscription, no dashboard.

## What it does

### Find things, and know where they came from

| | |
|---|---|
| **Answers with sources** | Ask a question and the answer arrives with **numbered citations** you can open. You can also ask it to answer only from a page, a PDF or your own notes. |
| **Search that simply works** | Web search is set up and working the moment you install — no key, no account, no configuration. It looks for a search service on your own machine first, then uses public ones. |
| **You choose how it thinks** | Five answer modes, one click each: **Flow** (a normal, well-paced answer), **Search** (look things up first), **Quality** (deep, careful answers), **Math** (worked step by step, with proper formulas) and **Replicate** (rebuild a new technique on your own data). |
| **Bring any AI you like** | 17 providers are built in — OpenAI, Anthropic, Google Gemini, Groq, Mistral, xAI, DeepSeek, Kimi, GLM, MiniMax, NVIDIA, OpenRouter, Together and Inception's Mercury — plus **Ollama** and **LM Studio** for models that run on your own machine, and a "custom" slot for anything else. |
| **Free models first** | Turn on **Free routing** and every question automatically goes to a free model you already have access to. If that model is busy, it quietly tries the next one. |
| **See the cost** | Every answer reports how many tokens it used, so a month of research never surprises you. You can also set how long the app should wait for each provider before giving up. |
| **Attach anything** | Drop in images for models that can see, PDFs and Word documents to read, and pick files from your own computer to use as context. |
| **Feeds that read like feeds** | Open an RSS or Atom feed and the reader typesets it — channel, items, dates and text — instead of showing you a wall of XML. |
| **Pages that resolve to the right page** | The reader follows stable article ids, so a search result always opens the article it says it opens. |

### Study it properly — Read & Quiz

| | |
|---|---|
| **Turn a paper into a study session** | Upload a PDF, Word file, text or Markdown document. You get a clean reading view with highlights, an AI summary, and a **quiz generated from the actual text**. |
| **Six question types** | Multiple choice, true/false, fill in the blank, short answer, put it in order, and match the pairs — with a timer, streaks and a score you can act on. |
| **Marking that feels instant** | Select any sentence to highlight it immediately — the mark sweeps on like a marker — with two buttons for what you probably want next (*quiz this passage*, *undo*). |
| **Steps that look like steps** | Lines meant as steps, lists and "Step 2 —" runs are turned into properly numbered steps and bullets **as you read them**, at no extra cost and with no extra AI call. |
| **Practice exams for 12 countries** | **Learn → Exam** runs a practice civics test built from the country you pick (the United States, Indonesia and ten more). While it runs, ResearchEngine is locked until you finish. It is practice material, not an official test. The lock is inside this app, so other programs stay open. |
| **Revise against the clock** | A study timer lives in the top bar and knows what it is timing — Quiz, Reading, or Coding — with pause, play, skip and reset. |

### Write it up

| | |
|---|---|
| **Articles** | A long-form editor with AI blocks, headings, quotes and **real images fetched from Wikimedia Commons**, so a draft is presentable without leaving the app. |
| **Notebooks** | Keep a private notebook per project and chat against *only* what is in it — your sources, not the whole internet. |
| **Share Studio** | Turn any answer or quiz result into a picture you can post: **Liquid glass**, **Gradient**, **Solid**, or your own background image. Copy it to the clipboard or save it. |
| **Your own voice** | One click of **Humanize** rewrites a reply so it reads like a person wrote it — uneven rhythm, plain words, no chatbot style — while every fact, number and citation stays exactly where it was. The rewrite shows in green, and the original waits in red behind a hover. |
| **Export everything** | A versioned ZIP of your whole library, and an import that merges it back. |

### Send a team of agents — Agent Flood

Point a swarm of assistants at one topic and let them work in parallel. Pick a squad (Deep Research, Software Design, and more), give them an objective, and watch them file findings into a shared scratchpad while a budget keeps the cost visible. When they are done you get one synthesised briefing instead of five separate answers.

### Put your routine on rails — Workflows

A visual canvas for anything you do repeatedly: a trigger, a few AI steps, some logic, a transform, an output. Build it once with drag and drop, run it whenever, read the run history, and share a workflow as a single file.

### Build things — Coding & Design workspaces

For the days when research turns into making something:

- **Coding workspace** — your project's files in a real editor with an AI pair who edits alongside you, runs commands behind an approval gate, and keeps a record of every change it makes. Files wear their language's own mark (JavaScript, TypeScript, React, Python, C++, Rust, Go and more) instead of one grey page icon.
- **Dependencies, visible** — a panel reads the open project's package.json: what is installed, what is declared, whether a lockfile pins it — next to a curated list of the dependencies people usually build AI features with, each one copy away.
- **MCP servers, one click** — recommended Model Context Protocol servers (filesystem, fetch, memory, GitHub, Puppeteer, search…) install straight from the coding workspace, are tested against the live bridge, and link out to the open MCP registries for everything else.
- **Screenshot to code** — drop an image of an interface and a vision model writes it back as code: a single HTML file with Tailwind, or a React component. Screen recordings work too: the panel samples frames across the video and rebuilds the final state. Copy the result or save it into the project.
- **Design mode** — the app in front of you *shows itself* to the AI: it can read the real layout, colours and console, take screenshots, click and type through your screen, and then prove a fix with an assertion. You can also press every control yourself as a one-click test.
- **UI components library** — browse ready-made components from the popular registries, preview them live inside design mode, save the ones you like, and copy one command that installs everything you saved.
- **Start from nothing** — create a project, upload a ZIP, or press **Try a sample app** for a ready-made page with deliberate bugs to play with.

**Changes, like a source client.** The coding dock's Changes tab lists unstaged and staged files with their line counts, opens each diff with line numbers and added lines in green, and offers stage all, revert all (a second click confirms it, because it discards work) and a commit. Nothing is pushed.

### Connect your own accounts

Research Engine can read the places your work already lives — **Google Drive, Gmail, Google Calendar, GitHub, Notion, Slack**, and files on your own computer. Connect an account by pasting a token *once*, and the app **tests it against the real service before trusting it** and tells you which account it is signed in as.

Once connected, you can search all of it from the chatbox and attach a result to your question — and the assistant can look things up itself, asking you to connect an account instead of inventing what might be in it. Your tokens are encrypted on your machine and are never sent anywhere except the service they belong to.

### Talk to it

Dictation works with three engines — your browser's own, a transcription service you already have an account with, or a **Whisper server on your own machine** for anything confidential. Hold the mic to talk or click it to start and stop, watch the glow around the chatbox move with your voice, and your words land in the composer ready to send.

### Run several sessions at once

Every chat streams on its own, so one can think while you read another. Press **Alt + Shift** (rebindable in Settings → Shortcuts) and your sessions fly out as cards: press again to step through them, Enter to land. A session can run inside its own Docker container or a multipass VM when a task needs isolation, and the chat says so when it does.

### Effort, and thinking you can read

The composer's effort pill speaks each provider's own vocabulary: Claude's low, medium, high, xHigh and max, and ChatGPT's Instant through Max. Models that think show their chain of thought in a collapsible block above the answer. For Claude the app asks for a summary of the reasoning, never the raw chain of thought.

### Bring your chats with you

Settings → Data imports a ChatGPT export (the zip, or its conversations.json) or a Claude export. Each conversation becomes a session in your history, read entirely in your browser. For ChatGPT the branch you ended on is the one that comes across.

### Make it work your way

- **Skills** — reusable standards you can put on any task. Three ship with the app: a **design pass** that scores its own work and must clear 8/10, a **zero-hallucination pass** that verifies every claim against the real project, and a **no-filler pass** that refuses to leave TODOs and placeholders behind. Write your own in plain language.
- **Personas** — 836 system prompts from the public archive of frontier-product prompts. The flagship ones open offline; Settings → Personas syncs the full archive once. Attach one to a chat and the model answers in its voice, on top of your own instructions.
- **Skill packs** — the full Apple-design pack from emilkowalski/skills, and the annotated-illustration pack with an adapter for models other than Codex. Attach them per chat.
- **MCP servers** — Settings → MCP Servers and the coding panel share one list. The catalog holds 7,729 servers from an open index; the 116 with a verified run command install in one click, and the rest open their project page. mcpservers.org lists more, and links out from the panel.
- **Master prompts** — the instructions behind every answer are readable and versioned. Change them, compare them, roll them back.
- **Tasks** — a checklist you share with the assistant, pinned to the top so the AI works on what you actually care about.
- **Meetings, on your device** — Library → Meetings records a conversation from your microphone. Text appears in about 20-second parts as people talk, transcribed on this computer with Whisper. Type notes alongside the recording; they are saved with the meeting and used when you write notes. Pick a template (general, 1:1, interview, lecture or study session) and the notes follow its sections. Where the microphone was lost, the transcript says so with a timed line, instead of showing a silent gap. Leaving the Library tab does not stop a recording. The recording stays in your browser's storage until you delete it. Speakers are not separated, and the app does not join calls for you. The first transcription downloads the speech model from the Hugging Face hub, once.
- **YouTube in the Library** — Library → Videos keeps a video with its title, your notes and a start time. Clicking a video opens it in a popup player inside the app, through YouTube's privacy-enhanced embed. Learn → Videos finds videos for what you are studying, and any of them can be saved to your Library with one click.
- **Calendar, on your device** — the Calendar page shows a month grid and an agenda. Events you make stay in this browser. You can also bring in a calendar as an .ics file, or add a Google Calendar by its *secret address in iCal format* (Google Calendar → calendar settings → Integrate calendar). That address is read-only; this app fetches it through its own backend, and only Google's calendar host is allowed. Recurring events are expanded for daily, weekly, monthly and yearly rules. Export your events as one .ics file that Google Calendar can import. There is no Google sign-in, so nothing is written back to Google.
- **Bots** — a Bot is a named teammate with a job, a face (18 shapes from bot-avatars, MIT), its own thread, and a memory you can see and edit (type `remember: …` to save something). It can run routines on a schedule (at least five minutes apart, up to 50 per bot, the last 20 runs kept). Replies come from the model you have configured. Routines run only while Research Engine is open, and *Pause all routines* stops them without touching chats. Design notes are in `docs/research/grok-bot-notes.md`.
- **Bots on the web, with the steps shown** — switch *Web access* on for a bot and it can search and open pages. Every step it took appears under its reply as *How it answered*, and the pages it read are listed as sources. Search goes to **SearXNG**, which we recommend running yourself with Docker (`docker run -d -p 8080:8080 searxng/searxng`, then paste `http://localhost:8080` in Bots → Settings; enable the JSON format in its `settings.yml`). Pages are read directly by default. For JavaScript-heavy sites, run a headless browser container such as `browserless/chrome` and paste its address; it must expose `POST /content`.
- **Group chats and handoffs** — put two to six bots in a group chat. The first member answers your message, and a bot calls on the next teammate with `@Name`; a round stops after six bot turns, when nobody is called, or when a bot passes. A **project** has a brief and a log, and its owner can hand it to another bot: the old owner writes a brief for the new one, and the log keeps who had it and when.
- **Move your bots** — Bots → Settings exports every bot, group and project to one JSON file (threads and memories included, in plain text), and imports one without overwriting what is already here.
- **Agent roles from ECC** — 16 skills and 8 agent roles from [affaan-m/ecc](https://github.com/affaan-m/ecc) (MIT) are built in. The skills join the chat's skill packs; a role gives a bot its working method when you start it. Vendored at one pinned commit; see `docs/research/ecc-integration.md`.
- **Photos you send together** — when one message has two or more photos, they open in a carousel with arrows and dots, not as a stack of full-size images.
- **Backgrounds that move** — Settings → Appearance adds two backgrounds: an animated silk-like gradient, and *Etched Accretion*, a black hole in crimson and silver that drifts toward the pointer and flares when you press. Both run behind the home screen and the chats.
- **A streak for real work** — the home screen shows your streak as a week of ticks, and today's focus minutes toward a goal. A minute counts only when the tab is visible, you have pressed, typed or scrolled in the last two minutes, and the view is one where you are working: a lesson, coding, reading a source, writing or a workflow. Five active minutes keep the day; 25 and 60 minutes in a day earn badges, and streak milestones (3, 7, 14, 30, 100 days) earn a badge and XP. Nothing is counted while the app sits open on a page with nothing going on.
- **Learn → Path has a demo** — no document needed. *See the demo* builds a sample path about photosynthesis on this device, and nothing from it is saved to your library.
- **Two looks for your folders** — the Library shelf shows folders as blue SmoothUI folders, or as frosted-glass folders that lean toward your pointer and fan out their files when opened. Pick the look above the shelf; it is remembered on this device.
- **Developer bug finder** — with developer mode on in Settings → Developer, press **Alt+Shift+B** to pick any element on screen. It writes a bug report with the page, the element (its selector and text), and a line of your own. You can attach a screenshot you took yourself; the app does not take screenshots.
- **Make it beautiful** — light, dark, Catppuccin, Solarized, Nord, high-contrast and more; gradients that animate into a new theme instead of snapping; glass, frosted or solid surfaces; your own background image; a dock-style sidebar; and a profile avatar generated from your name.
- **Privacy Shield** — three postures in one switch: **No Privacy**, **Privated** (the default — secrets are scrubbed from anything that leaves your machine), or **Top Secret** (nothing leaves except your chosen AI provider; search runs offline from what you already have).
- **Feels like an app, not a website** — a command palette (`Ctrl/Cmd + K`), right-click menus everywhere, animated page changes, live toasts, a scroll indicator, and a desktop app that opens like any other program.

### Keeps itself up to date

Press update and it fetches the new version, **checks the file against its published fingerprint before unpacking anything**, installs it, and — if the new version cannot start — puts the old one back by itself. Your library is never part of that swap.

**Before any update starts, your data is backed up.** The desktop app copies the database (with a consistent snapshot, even while the app is open) and your settings into a `backups/` folder inside your data folder, and keeps the newest five. If that copy cannot be made, the update does not start. The desktop installer keeps the same app identity, so an update is meant to install over the old version, and your data folder is outside the program. Neither the Windows nor the Linux in-place upgrade has been tried on a machine with an older install yet; CI builds both installers, and the backup logic is tested. (You can also hand it a file you downloaded yourself, or point it at a private source if you run a fork.)

**And it tells you.** When a newer release exists, it says so — once per version, in a popup that shows the release notes, the download and its progress, with a restart when it is done and a "skip this version" that is remembered. Nothing installs without your press.

## Only in Research Engine

Plenty of apps answer questions. These are the parts you will not find elsewhere — the reason this app exists:

1. **Answers that are written, not dumped.** A pacing engine shapes the reply as it streams — clause by clause, with pauses at punctuation and whole-code-block reveals — so a long answer reads like prose instead of a slot machine. Four settings, from raw to cinematic.
2. **Study text fixed by the app, not by another AI call.** `:dot:` runs, "Step 2 —" and repeated numbering become real numbered steps and nested bullets *at the moment they are displayed*. No extra request, no extra cost, no chance of the model rewriting your source.
3. **Humanize that keeps the facts.** One click rewrites a reply in human prose and shows you both versions — the new text in green, the old one behind a hover — with numbers and citations untouched. Chat only, never inside code.
4. **A quiz engine, not a quiz toy.** Six question types generated from your own document, with a timer, streaks, a shareable result card, and a study timer that knows you are in Quiz mode.
5. **Agent Flood.** A squad of agents on one objective, working in parallel, filing into a shared scratchpad, under a visible token budget, ending in one synthesised briefing — on a laptop.
6. **A design workspace where the AI can actually see the screen.** It reads real geometry, colours and console output, takes screenshots, clicks and types through your running app, resizes it to phone widths, and then *proves* a fix with an assertion. You can press every control yourself as a one-click test.
7. **Screenshot to code, with video.** An interface on film becomes working code: images map straight to HTML/Tailwind or React, and a recording is sampled into frames so the AI rebuilds what it ends on.
8. **MCP as a one-click shelf.** The coding workspace recommends the tool servers worth having, installs them into your config, tests them live, and points at the registries for the rest — nothing buried in settings.
9. **Free routing.** Your questions go to a free model you already have — a local one first — and move on by themselves when a model is busy. Not a provider you have to choose, just a switch.
10. **An updater that works for a private project.** A read-only token or a plain manifest link, the fingerprint checked *before* a single byte is unpacked, and an automatic rollback if the new version cannot start.
11. **Integrations the assistant can actually use.** Real accounts with real sign-in — Drive, Gmail, Calendar, GitHub, Notion, Slack, your own files — searchable from the chatbox and readable by the agent, with "you have not connected this yet" as an answer instead of a made-up one.
12. **Your work, one ZIP away.** Notes, chats, quizzes, articles, workflows and skills all live in a single folder on your disk. Back it up by copying it. Move it to a new computer by copying it. Nobody can revoke your access to your own work.
13. **Everything local-first, with a shield.** Encrypted keys, no account, no telemetry, and three privacy postures that decide what is even allowed to leave the machine.
14. **And a workspace, not a chat box.** Reader, quiz, notebooks, articles, agent teams, workflows, a coding desk and a design studio — in one app you own.

## Your work stays yours

Everything Research Engine writes lives in **one folder** on the machine running it:

| Where | What is in it |
|---|---|
| `~/.research-engine/` (Windows: `%USERPROFILE%\.research-engine`) | Your whole library: chats, notebooks, documents, quizzes, articles, skills, workflows |
| `keys.enc` | Your provider keys, encrypted with your passphrase |
| `backups/` | Snapshots taken before each update (newest five kept) |
| Your own backups | A versioned ZIP from **Settings → Data**, or just copy the folder |

**Locking your keys.** Two layers protect the key vault. The **vault passphrase** encrypts every provider key (AES-GCM, with a key derived by PBKDF2). The optional **authenticator lock** (Settings → Security) adds a six-digit code from an app such as Google Authenticator, Stratum, Aegis or 1Password: unlocking the vault then takes the passphrase *and* the current code. Setup shows a QR code, and the same key typed by hand for apps that cannot scan. The code must be typed once to confirm the setup, so a bad setup cannot lock you out, and five wrong codes make the box wait 30 seconds.

Be clear about the limit: the authenticator secret lives on this device, encrypted with your passphrase. It stops someone who only has the device, or only has the passphrase, from using your keys. It does not stop someone who has both the device and the passphrase. A second device or a hardware key would be the stronger version, and it is not part of this release.

**What leaves your computer:** the messages needed to get an answer, sent to the AI provider *you* configured; your search queries to the search service you chose (SearXNG at the address you set, or the public instances if you set none); the pages a bot with web access opens, fetched from the sites themselves or through the browser container you run; and, only if you use them, these: saving a YouTube link asks YouTube for the video's title and shows its thumbnail, playing a video loads YouTube's embed, and the first meeting transcription downloads the Whisper model from the Hugging Face hub, and adding a Google Calendar address makes this computer fetch that calendar from Google once per sync (the address is kept only in this browser). Meeting audio is never sent anywhere. A transcript goes to your configured model only when you press *Write notes*. That is the entire list. No telemetry, no analytics, no crash reports, no "checking for updates" pings that carry your data.

**What never leaves:** your library, your files, your keys, and — with **Privacy Shield** on its default setting — anything that looks like a secret, which is scrubbed before an outbound message is allowed to include it.

The desktop app, a browser tab and a portable copy on the same machine all read the same library. Deleting the app never deletes your data.

## Questions people ask

**Do I need an AI account?** Not to try it — the built-in offline model runs every part of the app so you can explore. For real answers you add a provider you already pay for, or a model on your own machine, and you pay them directly instead of paying us.

**Is my work private?** It stays on your computer. Nothing is uploaded to us because there is no "us" server — the app and your library live entirely on your machine. See [Your work stays yours](#-your-work-stays-yours).

**Is this like Perplexity?** The answer experience is comparable — questions in, sourced answers out — but this one runs locally, keeps your library on your disk, and adds a study desk, a writing room, agent teams and a design studio. (The bundled "Perplexity" theme is an affectionate look-alike, nothing more; this is an independent project.)

**What does "beta" mean here?** It works, it is tested, and it may still change shape between small versions. Take a backup from Settings → Data now and then and you are covered.

**Can several people share one copy?** Yes. Run it once on a home server or a spare computer and everyone opens it in a browser, each with their own screen and their own chats, sharing one library.

**Does it work offline?** With the built-in model and content you already have, yes — entirely. Live search and paid models need a connection, by definition.

**Something is broken / it feels old.** Press **Settings → About**. It shows exactly which version you are running and whether a newer one exists. If you are on an older build, press update; if you built it yourself, it tells you the one command to run.
